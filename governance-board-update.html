Why This Project Exists

⏱ ~3 minutes
Tenants get a modern, self-service repairs experience; tradespeople get a system that fits how they actually work in the field — not a desk-bound process forced onto a mobile job.

The Problem Today

  • No tenant self-service — repairs reported by phone only, no status visibility
  • Disconnected systems — data rekeyed between platforms, no single source of truth
  • Field staff working largely offline — job updates batch-processed at day end
  • Multiple, disconnected sign-ins slow tradespeople down before they even start a job

What Success Looks Like

  • A repairs and works management system that fits real field conditions, not office assumptions
  • One straightforward sign-in for tradespeople to reach everything they need on a job
  • Joined-up data across repairs, stores, and finance — one version of the truth
  • A programme that is governed, evidenced, and accountable at every stage

Governance Structure

⏱ ~2 minutes

Three Groups, Staggered Cadence

Project GroupPrimary governance body, chaired by the Project Owner. Strategic direction, corporate alignment, accountability for outcomes, and approval of major changes to scope, budget, or timeline.
Steering GroupCross-functional delivery oversight — progress, workstream risks, and coordination across repairs, finance, stores, customer services, HR, and systems. First point of escalation before the Project Group.
Technical GroupWhere systems, data, and integration detail live — IT and specialist input without requiring every service area to sit through it.

Why It's Structured This Way

  • Escalation flows one direction — Technical → Steering → Project — so each group is briefed by the one below it
  • Membership spans every service actually affected: repairs, finance, HR, ICT, procurement, business improvement, information governance, and community benefits

This Board's Role Today

  • Assurance that the programme is progressing on a sound footing
  • Decisions on the specific asks later in this update
  • A channel to raise anything not yet visible to the delivery team

Mobile Device Workflows

⏱ ~3 minutes
A tradesperson standing at a job should be able to get straight to what they need — not work through several separate sign-ins first.

The Problem

  • Tradespeople currently sign in separately to multiple systems just to start a job
  • Each extra sign-in is friction in the field — time lost, and a barrier to adoption
  • The new repairs system needs to fit into this device and identity picture from day one, not be bolted on afterwards

The Direction of Travel

  • Better use of Microsoft Intune for device management and conditional access — so a managed device carries trusted, streamlined sign-in with it
  • Single sign-on wherever the underlying systems support it, rather than a separate username and password per app
  • Design this as part of the ROCC discovery work, not as a separate retrofit

Working With Other App Providers

⏱ ~3 minutes

Tradespeople don't only need the repairs system — they also touch HR and other business applications during a normal working day. A joined-up sign-in only works if those system owners are involved, not just ROCC and IT.

Who Needs to Be at the Table

  • HR — for the systems tradespeople use day-to-day (rostering, absence, payroll-adjacent tools)
  • Other business application owners — any further system a tradesperson has to sign into as part of a normal shift
  • IT / Identity team — to confirm what Intune and single sign-on can realistically support across this list

Why This Can't Wait

  • Identity and access design is far cheaper to get right once, up front, than to retrofit after ROCC is configured
  • Every system left out of this conversation becomes another separate sign-in for tradespeople in the field
  • This needs to run in parallel with the ROCC discovery workshops, not after them
