Operational Teams

Teams engaged through discovery sessions, walkthroughs, and requirement-gathering workshops.

Customer Services — Repairs

Front-line contact centre managing tenant repair requests

Active — ongoing

Requirements captured

  • Significant reduction in spreadsheet use
  • Ability to upload images alongside job comments
  • Inspection plans visible within the system
  • Property information and attributes surfaced — factored status, asbestos, APEX data

Agreed next steps

  • Ad hoc meetings as requirements evolve
  • Participation in future design workshops
  • Input to steering group activities

Stores

Parts and materials supply for housing repairs and maintenance

Active — multiple sessions

Requirements captured

  • Orders pre-built and ready to collect — not generated face-to-face with tradesmen
  • Orders auto-generated based on job requirements
  • Reduced spreadsheet use and duplication of data entry
  • Auto-replenishment of stock based on current levels and demand
  • Stock prices set in advance and activated when a contract comes into effect

Agreed next steps

  • Further discovery and requirements workshops
  • Involvement in steering groups as required
  • Proposed: project manager to spend a day in Stores using live systems

Stores requirements closely mirror those of Customer Services — both teams want to eliminate duplicated manual data entry and reduce reliance on spreadsheets. The systems integration work needs to serve both teams together.

Business Support Team

Finance, invoicing, and administrative support for the repairs service

Active — multiple sessions

Requirements captured

  • High volume of manual workflows — primary target for automation and process redesign
  • Requirements are detailed and nuanced; captured fully in process maps
  • Integra (billing and invoicing) integration is critical — must be right

Agreed next steps

  • Continued individual and group sessions
  • Process maps in progress and held in SharePoint (ENV/99/24)
  • Integra integration to be explored in depth through ROCC discovery

Integra integration: North Ayrshire Council (ROCC users visited in discovery) don't use Integra as extensively and report no issues. Other organisations integrate the two systems successfully and speak highly of how ROCC manages the interface. This will be explored further when we meet with ROCC directly.

Housing Maintenance Team (HMT)

Tradesmen, supervisors, and management responsible for delivering repairs

Active — ongoing

Topics and requirements covered

  • Voids management — team keen to develop this area specifically
  • Mobile working for tradesmen in the field
  • Reporting and performance visibility
  • Schedule of Rates (SORs)
  • Van stock management
  • Mobile phone rollout — managed access to software for full team

Agreed next steps

  • Dedicated voids workshop to be arranged
  • Joint workshops with Stores and Trades to be co-ordinated
  • HMT Manager joining the Programme Board
  • Further team members on Technical Board and Project Board

The programme has already managed the mobile phone rollout for the full HMT, ensuring devices and software are in place for a smooth transition to the new system. This is an early, tangible delivery win from the discovery phase.

Project Team Development

The core team is being built up alongside delivery — combining housing and business expertise with digital capability built from the ground up.

Core Project Team

Project Manager + two Project Support Officers

Capability building — ongoing

The project support officers bring deep expertise in business support, corporate services, repairs, maintenance, and housing — alongside a strong understanding of how the Council operates as a whole. Digital project management is a new discipline for the team and has been introduced progressively alongside live delivery.

Topics introduced in the first two months

Agile delivery & the Agile Manifesto
Poka Yoke (mistake-proofing)
Kanban and visual management
Databases, schemas, and data structures
APIs and authentication
The web and web safety
Microsoft 365 as a connected delivery tool
Needs-led design principles
Gestalt principles for collaborative work
Scottish digital landscape and policy
Networking and knowledge-finding

All of this capability-building has happened alongside live delivery activity — not instead of it. The team has been mapping processes, attending discovery sessions, building tools, and engaging stakeholders at the same time as developing their digital knowledge.

Organisational Learning

Understanding how ERC operates is itself part of the discovery work.

ERC is a complex organisation — services, systems, and teams are often siloed, and information about APIs, system documentation, and data ownership is not always easy to locate. Part of the project manager's role in this phase has been active networking to surface the right people and connect the right information.

Activities undertaken

  • Joined the ERC Change Agent Forum
  • Identified who is responsible for address and phone number data standardisation
  • API and system documentation research across NECH, DRS, Integra, APEX, ROCC
  • Met with North Ayrshire Council to understand ROCC in use
  • Active networking across services and departments

Why this matters

  • Systems integration depends on knowing who owns each data source
  • Future design decisions will be grounded in how ERC actually operates — not how documentation says it does
  • Relationships built now will support co-design workshops, testing, and change management later

What the engagement picture tells us

Every team engaged so far has been positive and willing. There are clear, consistent themes across all four teams — reduce manual work, eliminate spreadsheets, connect data between systems, and make information visible to the right people at the right time.

These themes will directly shape the requirements baseline, vendor evaluation criteria, and co-design activities in the next phase.
