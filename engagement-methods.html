Tenant Engagement — The Plan

Our Tenant Engagement Plan

This page sets out how we intend to engage with ERC's 3,141 housing tenants throughout the Housing Digital Transformation programme. It is structured around the Double Diamond: engagement during discovery (understanding the problem), during design (shaping the solution), during testing (validating before build), and after go-live (measuring what has actually changed). It is a living document and will be updated as the programme progresses.

Status: Discovery phase — engagement underway
Scope: All ERC housing tenants; targeted outreach for seldom-heard groups

The guiding principle for this plan

Every engagement activity in this plan must produce something that directly influences a programme decision. If we cannot point to a specific design choice, a changed requirement, or a modified approach that resulted from a piece of tenant engagement, that engagement did not serve its purpose. We are not collecting tenant views to report that we collected them — we are collecting them to build better services.

Engagement Already Underway

Alongside the planned activities below, the programme has already begun reaching tenants. These are live discovery activities — not future commitments.

Recent repairs follow-up

We extracted a list of repairs from the last 30 days in the current repairs system and are contacting those tenants by phone. The conversations focus on their experience of the full journey — from reporting the repair through to the job being completed.

We are also using these contacts to introduce the Housing Digital Transformation programme and ask whether tenants would like to be involved in helping steer how the new service is developed. This gives us real, recent experience data from tenants who have just been through the process we are redesigning.

Status: In progress

East Renfrewshire-wide publication

We have published an article in an East Renfrewshire-wide council publication, providing information on the Housing Digital Transformation programme and what it means for tenants.

The article invites tenants to get in touch if they want to be involved in shaping the programme, or if they have experiences and suggestions they would like to share. This is an open invitation — not limited to tenants on the recent repairs list — and helps us reach people who may not have had a recent housing contact but still have views worth hearing.

Status: Published; responses being followed up

Who We Need to Reach

ERC's housing stock is not homogenous. A single engagement channel will not reach all tenants, and the tenants hardest to reach are often those with the most at stake in a digital transformation. The plan needs to actively target underrepresented groups.

High-volume service users

Tenants who contact housing services frequently — repairs, rent, housing management queries. These tenants know the current process in detail, including where it fails. Their insight is the most valuable for understanding the problem. They are also the group for whom improved digital services will make the most practical difference.

Digitally excluded tenants

Older tenants, tenants with disabilities, tenants with low digital confidence or no internet access. This group is at highest risk of being made worse off by a digital-first approach. They require specific research to understand their needs and specific design decisions to ensure they are not left behind. ERC's Digital Champions network is the primary resource for reaching this group.

Seldom-heard groups

Tenants from minority ethnic communities, tenants with mental health needs, young tenants, and tenants in temporary accommodation. These groups are rarely well-represented in traditional tenant engagement. Their absence from the research does not mean their needs are met — it means we do not know whether they are met. Targeted outreach is required.

Phase 2 — Design: Shaping Solutions with Tenants

Method What it produces Who Format
Tenant Design Panel An ongoing reference group of tenants who review and respond to design proposals throughout the programme. Not a consultation body — a design partner. 10–12 tenants, recruited to reflect the diversity of ERC's housing stock. Paid reasonable expenses. Monthly meetings (in person and online), with between-meeting feedback via email or short surveys.
Co-design workshops Tenant-generated ideas and priorities for specific features: how appointment booking should work, what repair status updates should say, what the portal homepage should show first Mixed groups of tenants and housing staff working on specific design questions Two-hour facilitated sessions for each major feature area. Prototypes and outputs shared back.
Feedback on draft communications Tenant review of all tenant-facing communications produced by the programme — letters, SMS templates, portal copy, notification messages — before they are finalised Tenant Design Panel plus targeted review with plain-English and accessibility leads Read-aloud sessions, written feedback, online annotation tools.
Accessibility reviews Direct testing of designs with tenants who have visual, cognitive, or motor impairments to ensure digital services are genuinely accessible, not just technically compliant Tenants with relevant needs, supported by ERC's equalities networks Individual sessions with assistive technology; findings reported to design team.

Phase 3 — Testing: Validating Before Go-Live

Method What it produces Who Format
Remote usability testing Observed evidence of how tenants actually interact with the portal prototype — where they get confused, what they misunderstand, what they find easy. Directly informs final changes before go-live. 8–10 tenants per round; at least two rounds before launch Screen-share via Teams, moderated by a trained user researcher. Sessions recorded with consent.
In-person assisted testing Usability evidence from tenants who will not or cannot participate in remote testing — critical for understanding the digitally excluded experience before go-live Older tenants, tenants with low digital confidence, tenants with disabilities One-to-one sessions in community venues, libraries, or tenant homes. Supported by Digital Champions.
Pilot with volunteer tenants Real-world evidence from a small group using the live system before full rollout — surfaces issues that lab testing misses 20–30 volunteer tenants across different property types and demographics Supported rollout with fortnightly check-ins and a dedicated feedback channel. Issues resolved before general launch.
Tenant Satisfaction Snapshot A baseline satisfaction measure taken before go-live, so improvements after launch can be measured against a known starting point All housing tenants, via Citizens Panel and direct mail to non-panel members Short survey (5–8 questions) on satisfaction with repairs, communication, and housing management.

Phase 4 — Post Go-Live: Measuring What Has Changed

Method What it measures Frequency
Post-transaction feedback Tenant satisfaction immediately after completing a transaction in the portal — did they find what they needed, was the process clear, did it resolve their issue? Continuous, built into the portal
Quarterly satisfaction survey Broader satisfaction with housing services, tracking change from the pre-go-live baseline. Tracks adoption rates, channel preferences, and experience of specific services. Quarterly for first year; annually thereafter
Tenant Design Panel review Structured feedback from the Tenant Design Panel on how the live service compares to what was intended; identification of gaps and improvement priorities Three months post go-live, then six-monthly
Contact pattern analysis Are tenants calling less because the portal resolves their needs, or calling more because they are confused? Channel shift data is the clearest measure of whether the digital service is actually working. Monthly, reviewed by programme board

The Digital Inclusion Challenge

Digital inclusion is not a separate workstream from the engagement plan — it is woven through every phase of it. ERC already has infrastructure and experience in this area through the Connecting Scotland programme and the Digital Champions network. The Housing Digital Transformation Project will draw directly on both.

Digital Champions in housing

ERC's 30+ Digital Champions are primarily deployed across council services to support staff. For the Housing Digital Transformation Project, a subset of Champions with housing context should be designated as tenant-facing champions — able to support tenants in using the portal, provide in-person guidance, and feed back to the programme team on where tenants are struggling.

This mirrors the model that worked in the Connecting Scotland programme, where every device recipient had access to a Digital Champion. The relationship between champion and tenant creates continuity and trust that a helpline cannot replicate.

Maintaining non-digital channels

The programme must not remove or degrade the telephone and in-person channels that digitally excluded tenants depend on. The business case for digital transformation rests on channel shift for those who can and want to shift — it does not rest on forcing tenants who cannot or will not use digital channels into a worse service.

This is both the right approach and the legally required one. The design principle is: make digital the most convenient choice, not the only choice.

How Tenant Feedback Shapes the Programme

Every engagement activity feeds into the programme through a defined route. Tenant feedback does not disappear into a report — it connects to specific decisions.

Engagement activity Feeds into Decision it informs
Recent repairs follow-up Problem definition document; journey pain points; recruitment for Tenant Design Panel Which parts of the current repairs process fail tenants most often; priority features for the new service; who wants ongoing involvement
Programme publication responses Engagement register; discovery insights log Breadth of tenant interest in involvement; themes from unsolicited experiences and suggestions
Tenant interviews & journey mapping Problem definition document What the programme is solving for; system requirements; prioritisation of features
Citizens Panel baseline survey Programme board reporting; benefits framework Baseline satisfaction scores; target improvement levels; benefits realisation measurement
Tenant Design Panel Design decisions log Portal features, notification preferences, communications language, complaint process design
Usability testing Sprint backlog; go/no-go for launch Which usability issues must be resolved before go-live; which can follow in the next iteration
Digital inclusion audit Inclusion action plan Which non-digital channels to maintain; what support model is needed; champion deployment
Post-go-live satisfaction data Continuous improvement backlog Priority issues to address in post-launch iterations; evidence for the SHR regulatory return

Our Commitment to Tenants

We will tell tenants clearly what we are changing, why, and when. We will ask for their input before we build. We will show them what changed because of what they told us. We will make sure that tenants who cannot use digital services are not disadvantaged by this programme. And we will measure whether tenants' experience of their housing services has actually improved — not just whether the technology has gone live.
