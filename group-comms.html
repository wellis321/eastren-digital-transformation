How to use these messages

Step 1a — Don't know the project or me: Full introduction. Explains the programme, their role, and the group from scratch.

Step 1b — Know the project and me: Shorter, warmer message. Skips the background and gets straight to the point about the group and what's being asked.

Step 2 — Meeting invitation: Send either way once people have the context. Confirms the first meeting date and agenda. Click Copy on any message to copy it to your clipboard.

Step 1 — Introductory Messages

Send these first. Written for people who have no prior knowledge of the programme or their role in it.

Project Group

Introduction to the Programme & Your Role

Send before the meeting invitation — for members who haven't been briefed yet

Step 1 of 2
Hi [NAME], I wanted to get in touch to introduce myself and let you know about a programme I'm leading — and to ask for your involvement in it. I'm William Ellis, Digital Transformation Lead in Housing. I joined ERC earlier this year to lead the digital transformation of the Housing Repairs service. The background is this: the repairs service runs on Servitor, a legacy system that'll be unsupported from 2027. That gives us a fixed window to replace it — but rather than just swap one system for another, we're treating it as a chance to fundamentally redesign how the service is delivered. The programme will bring in mobile working for operatives and trades, replace Servitor with a modern platform, and connect the repairs service properly to the Council's core corporate systems. The goal is a service that's faster, more transparent, and a lot less reliant on spreadsheets and manual workarounds — better for tenants, and better for the people who run it. I'd like to invite you to join the Project Group for this programme. It's the main governance body — it meets every four weeks, chaired by the Project Owner, and its job is to keep us aligned with corporate priorities, make the decisions that need senior input, and act as the escalation point when things can't be resolved at a working level. Given your role and what you cover, your involvement matters — both for the decisions the group will need to make and for making sure the right parts of the organisation are genuinely behind this. I'll be in touch shortly with an invitation to our first meeting. In the meantime, feel free to get in touch if you'd like to know more. Thanks, William Ellis Digital Transformation Lead Housing Repairs Digital Transformation Programme East Renfrewshire Council

Technical Group

Introduction to the Programme & Your Role

Send before the meeting invitation — for members who haven't been briefed yet

Step 1 of 2
Hi [NAME], I wanted to get in touch to introduce myself and let you know about some work that's going to directly affect how you and your team work day-to-day — and where I'd really value your involvement. I'm William Ellis, Digital Transformation Lead in Housing. I joined ERC earlier this year to lead the digital transformation of the Housing Repairs service. The repairs service runs on Servitor — a legacy system that'll be unsupported from 2027. We're replacing it, but more than that, we're using it as a chance to properly redesign how the service is delivered. That means mobile working for operatives, better stock and job management, smarter scheduling, and systems that actually talk to each other rather than relying on spreadsheets and manual workarounds. I've already spent a lot of time with teams across repairs, stores, customer services, and business support, and what they've told me about what doesn't work and what they need has been really valuable. That kind of knowledge is what shapes a good solution. I'd like to invite you to join the Technical Group. It meets every four weeks and brings together the people who actually use the systems — to make sure that what we design and build works at an operational level. It's not a technical IT group. It's where frontline experience meets the design process. You don't need any project or digital background for this. What matters is that you know how the service works, what the problems are, and what better would look like. That's the knowledge that's hardest to get right without you in the room. I'll be in touch soon with the details of our first meeting. Feel free to get in touch in the meantime if you'd like to know more. Looking forward to working with you, William Ellis Digital Transformation Lead Housing Repairs Digital Transformation Programme East Renfrewshire Council

Full Meeting Schedule — First Cycle

All three groups run on a four-weekly cadence, staggered across the month.

Group 1st 2nd 3rd 4th
Technical Group 13 Jul 2026 10 Aug 2026 7 Sep 2026 5 Oct 2026
Steering Group 14 Jul 2026 11 Aug 2026 8 Sep 2026 6 Oct 2026
Project Group 22 Jul 2026 19 Aug 2026 16 Sep 2026 14 Oct 2026

Technical → Steering → Project within each four-week cycle. This sequencing means the Technical Group's operational view can inform the Steering Group, and the Steering Group's delivery picture can inform the Project Group — all within the same cycle.
