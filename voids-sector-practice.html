Voids — Sector Practice

How Other Landlords Are Tackling Void Turnaround

Scottish RSLs are ahead of most local authorities on void turnaround — averaging under 35 days against more than 57 for councils. That gap is not an accident. It comes from three deliberate design choices: categorising voids by complexity instead of treating every empty property the same, giving one person clear accountability for each case, and using data to see problems before they cause a long-term vacancy. The examples below are what that looks like in practice at two Glasgow-based RSLs, and the national data infrastructure that supports it.

Two Working Models

Maryhill Housing and ng homes take different approaches to the same problem — getting a property from “keys back” to “re-let” as fast as possible without compromising standards.

Maryhill Housing

Maryhill categorises every void into one of four target timescales based on the scale of work required, and pairs this with a financial incentive for tenants who leave the property in good condition.

  • Void categories — 3, 5, 10 and 12-day targets, from minimal cleaning up to properties needing new kitchens or bathrooms
  • Voids Incentive Scheme — up to £350 for tenants (leaving or transferring) who return the home in “very good condition”, reducing recharges and speeding categorisation into the fastest “3 Day Void” band
  • Reactive repair categories — Emergency (2hr response, 24hr fix), Urgent (24hr), Routine (5 working days) — used consistently across both tenanted and void stock

ng homes

ng homes runs a leaner incentive alongside a firm turnaround target, with eligibility tied tightly to tenant conduct during the notice period rather than the condition of the property alone.

  • 25 working day re-let target across the stock
  • Tenants Rewards Scheme — £50 for tenants who give 28 days’ written notice, allow access for inspections, repair damage they caused, return all keys, and clear any debt
  • Procedural focus — the reward is about following the correct end-of-tenancy process, which reduces the administrative and legal friction that otherwise slows a void down even when the property itself is in reasonable condition

Roles, Monitoring and Cross-Team Working

Both organisations rely on more than schemes and targets — they build accountability directly into staff structures.

Dedicated Roles

  • Void Property Officers — single point of accountability for managing contractors and overseeing the Lettable Standard on each case
  • Empty Homes Officers — in some associations, a separate role focused specifically on long-term, hard-to-let vacancies
  • Property Officers — conduct pre-inspections on high-value works (over £750) or structural defects, and post-inspections to check quality

Monitoring & Collaboration

  • Daily monitoring — Housing Officers track void progress every day to catch slippage before it compounds
  • Cross-departmental meetings — regular sessions between Housing and Property teams to resolve bottlenecks, particularly utility delays
  • Choice Based Lettings — a “property shop” model giving prospective tenants more say in their home, shown to improve tenancy sustainment and cut future turnover

The Data Infrastructure Behind It: Scotland's Housing Network

Both landlords — and most of the sector — sit inside a shared benchmarking and data ecosystem run by Scotland’s Housing Network (SHN), a membership body for local authorities and RSLs.

Why this matters for us

If ERC is not already using SHN benchmarking data on void performance, this is a low-cost, fast way to establish where our own turnaround times sit against comparable authorities — before committing to a specific digital or process solution.

  • Benchmarking tools — compare void turnover and re-let averages against peer groups and the national sector average
  • Business Intelligence (BI) tools — accurate, detailed comparisons through the reporting year, tracking trends in vacancy rates and rental loss
  • Charter Performance Insights — monitors performance against the Scottish Social Housing Charter, including average re-let times and percentage of rental income lost to void properties
  • Self-assessment support — frameworks to evaluate the efficiency of a landlord’s own void process, including identifying early warning signs of “low demand” properties
  • Knowledge exchange — structured sharing of best practice between members on reducing void periods and improving lettable standards
